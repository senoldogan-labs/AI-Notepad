Every major call app now writes meeting notes for you. Zoom has AI summaries built into its paid plans, Microsoft Teams has Copilot and intelligent recap, and Google Meet has Gemini's "Take notes for me". If your company already pays for one of them, it's fair to ask: why would you need anything else?

Often you don't. But the built-in notes have a pattern in common that matters more than any single feature, and it decides whether they're enough for you. Here's what each one does as of November 2026, and where the gaps are.

## Zoom: AI summaries in Zoom Workplace

Zoom's AI features (long sold as "AI Companion") include meeting summaries, smart recordings and AI notes. According to Zoom, these remain included with eligible paid Zoom Workplace plans. On the free Basic plan, the AI features are capped at a few meetings a month.

In June 2026 Zoom also launched **ZoomMate**, a separate paid product for agent-style tasks such as searching across your work data and completing follow-ups. You don't need it for basic meeting summaries.

How it works in practice:

- **The host turns it on.** Summaries are a host feature. Participants see a notice that the AI is active.
- **The summary goes to the host's account**, and the host decides who receives it.
- **It covers Zoom meetings.** It doesn't help with a meeting you join on Teams or Meet.

## Microsoft Teams: Copilot and intelligent recap

Teams has two layers:

- **Intelligent recap** gives AI-generated notes, chapters and suggested tasks after a recorded or transcribed meeting. It's part of Teams Premium, and Microsoft 365 Copilot includes it too.
- **Copilot in Teams meetings** lets you ask questions during and after the meeting ("What did we decide about the budget?") and draft follow-ups. It needs a **Microsoft 365 Copilot** license for each user, which is a paid add-on to a Microsoft 365 plan.

In 2026 Microsoft also brought recaps together in a dedicated Recap app in Teams, and added a way to get an AI recap without keeping the transcript, for organizations whose policies don't allow stored transcripts.

How it works in practice:

- **Your organization decides.** Admins control transcription, recording and Copilot policies, and licenses are assigned per person.
- **It usually needs transcription on.** No transcript, no recap, except where the transcript-free option is enabled.
- **It covers Teams meetings.** A client's Zoom call is outside its reach.

## Google Meet: "Take notes for me"

Gemini in Meet can take notes during a meeting and save them as a Google Doc, with a summary and suggested next steps. It's available on Business Standard and higher Workspace editions, and for personal accounts with Google AI Pro or Ultra. Business Starter doesn't include it.

How it works in practice:

- **The person who starts it owns the notes.** The Doc is saved in the meeting owner's Drive, and admins control how it's shared.
- **Language support is narrower than for translation.** In Google's most recent list we could find, "Take notes for me" supports eight spoken languages: English, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese and Spanish. Meet's speech translation, by comparison, now covers 63 (we compared the [live translation features](/blog/live-translation-zoom-teams-google-meet) separately).
- **It covers Google Meet.** Other call apps aren't included.

## The pattern: whose meeting is it?

Put the three side by side and the same thing shows up each time: **the built-in notes belong to the platform and to the meeting's owner.**

That's a sensible design for a company that runs every meeting on one platform and pays for the upgrade. It leaves gaps for a lot of people:

- **Guests.** In a partner's Teams meeting, what you get depends on your own license and on their settings, and the recording and transcript stay in their organization. In a Zoom or Meet meeting you don't host, you get the summary only if the host shares it.
- **Mixed platforms.** Freelancers, consultants, salespeople and anyone who works with other companies end up on all three apps in one week. Their notes end up in three places, in three formats, or not at all.
- **Languages.** If your meetings are in a language the built-in notes don't support well, you're back to typing.
- **Search across everything.** "What did we agree with that client in March?" is hard to answer when March's meetings are spread over three platforms and two companies' accounts.

## When the built-in notes are enough

Stick with what you have if:

- Nearly all your meetings are on one platform, in your own organization.
- Your company already pays for the AI tier.
- Your meetings are in a well-supported language.
- You're happy for notes to live in the platform (Zoom, Teams or Google Docs) and be governed by your admins.

That describes a lot of people, and adding another app would just be another app.

## When a separate note app makes sense

A note app that runs on your own computer, independent of the call platform, is worth it when:

- **You're often the guest**, not the host.
- **You use more than one call app**, or meet in person as well.
- **You want one searchable place** for all your meetings, wherever they happened.
- **Your meetings are in other languages**, or switch between them.
- **You'd rather keep notes on your own device** than in an organizer's cloud account.

There's one important rule if you do: a note app on your computer doesn't show up in the call, so telling people you're recording is your job. We wrote about [how to do that](/blog/meeting-notes-on-mac-without-a-bot). And if your company has a policy on AI tools, follow it; some organizations allow only the built-in option.

## The short version

- **Zoom:** AI summaries are included with eligible paid plans; the host turns them on and owns them.
- **Teams:** intelligent recap with Teams Premium or Copilot; Copilot in meetings needs a per-user license.
- **Meet:** "Take notes for me" on Business Standard and up, or Google AI Pro and Ultra; notes go to the owner's Drive.
- Built-in notes are enough if you mostly host meetings on one platform your company pays for.
- A separate app helps if you're often the guest, use several platforms or languages, or want one searchable place.
